## What passed

**The over-refusal guard holds.** A1, A2, B1, R1 and R5 all produced answers; none
came back `none`. The single `none` was R3, correctly — its SOP was not retrieved.
The coverage gate is not behaving as a blanket refusal in production.

**Reserved decisions survive into the steps.** 18 of 18. D1, R2, R4 and R5 — the
cases where the tester saw Ops told to add or change an ICD-10 code — all route the
choice to the prescriber and quote the whole governing rule, parenthetical included.

**Citations quote whole source units.** 17 of 17 scored rows.

**No truncation and no degenerate generations** across all 25 answers.

## Three real failures

**C3 — finding 4 is not closed for `partial` answers.** Asked how to change which
family member receives appointment reminders, Cortex declared `Coverage: partial`,
listed under _Not covered by the SOPs_ "The specific steps to update the appointment
reminder setup in Athena" — and then wrote four numbered steps doing exactly that
("Open the patient's chart in Athena", "Locate the section for caregiver or emergency
contact information", "Update the contact information"). Neither cited SOP describes
changing reminder recipients. The gate cannot catch this: it enforces the declaration
the model wrote, and the model declared `partial`, which permits steps. This is the
original C3 failure in a new shape.

**B1 — the closing question asks Ops for a clinical judgment.** "What is the patient's
diagnosis, to help the prescriber choose the correct ICD-10 code?" Hard rule 15
forbids asking for a choice a passage reserves for another role, and the diagnosis is
the input to exactly that choice. The rest of the answer is correct: it routes the
code to the prescriber and quotes the whole rule.

**B3 and C1 — the patient script never names the turnaround.** Both quote the 5–15
business-day prior-authorization window and then tell the patient only "We will keep
you updated on the status" (B3) or "let you know as soon as the scan is booked" (C1).
Hard rule 14 requires saying so in the script when a date cannot be met. The original
harm — quoting the turnaround and then planning a next-day scan — is not reproduced;
no script promises a date. But expectations are still not set.

Ungraded, found by reading: **C1 step 2 names an individual** ("Send a message to
Lindsay Powell") as the handler. Hard rule 12 requires roles, not people. No indicator
covers this.

## A reliability finding

Three of the first 18 answers (A2, B2, R1) returned no retrieval at all — zero chunks,
zero ranked files — and the reader got the no-match notice instead of an answer.
Re-probing `/search` for those three questions twice each returned 14, 15 and 8 chunks
with the expected SOP ranked first for B2 and R1. Re-running the three answers produced
full answers that passed every indicator.

So this is a transient empty response from AI Search on the answer path, not a
retrieval-quality problem. At 3 in 18 it is frequent enough that production users will
sometimes be told no SOP covers a question the corpus plainly answers. `searchWithRetry`
retries only on rate limits.

## Two grader corrections

Both were found by reading answers that had been marked failures and were not. Fixed
and pinned in `grade.test.ts`; the recorded D1/R2/R4/B1 failure shapes still fail.

- `gradeReserve` scored "Wait for the prescriber to select the code" (B1) and "Once the
  prescriber responds with the selected code, add it to the order" (T1) as Ops choosing
  a code. A role that has already decided now exempts the clause, but only when it
  precedes the verb — "Select the code, which the prescriber chooses" still fails. It
  also scored A1's "If the study name is incorrect, update it" as a code change; the
  clause itself must now mention a code.
- `gradeOneQuestion` failed R2's "What is the exact study name for the order?" on a bare
  `/name/`. Identity is now scoped to the person, so a facility's phone number or a
  study name passes while the patient's name or date of birth still fails.
